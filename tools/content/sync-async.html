The trade-off

Synchronous communication (meetings, calls, instant chat) gets quick answers and picks up tone and confusion on the spot. But it needs every participant's attention at the same time, and each interruption costs more than the minutes spent: deep work takes a long time to resume.

Asynchronous communication (documents, email, recorded updates) lets people reply when they're ready, scales across time zones, and creates a record. The costs are slower back-and-forth and a greater risk that a subtle point is misread and nobody notices for a day.

How to use it

  1. Default to async for information: status, announcements, FYIs.
  2. Go live for ambiguity and emotion: disagreements, sensitive feedback, anything that has gone back and forth more than twice.
  3. Write down what was decided after any live conversation. Otherwise the decision exists only in memory.
  4. Set response expectations ("by end of next day") so async doesn't mean "ignored".

Example

A weekly one-hour status meeting with ten people costs ten person-hours, for updates that fit in a short written summary. Replacing it with a document everyone reads saves most of that time. When two leads disagree over the plan in the document comments for the third round, a 15-minute call settles it.

Watch out for

  • Async that never closes. Without a deadline or an owner, threads drift and decisions don't get made.
  • Live by default. Constant availability looks responsive, but it's the utilisation trap applied to attention.
  • Pairs with brevity vs. completeness (writing well matters more when it's the only channel) and speed vs. consensus.
