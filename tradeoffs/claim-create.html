Claiming vs. creating value

In a negotiation you can grow what's on the table or fight over your share of it. Sharing information grows the pie but exposes you, and pushing hard for your share shrinks the pie and the chance of a deal.

Creating valuevs.Claiming value

The trade-off

Negotiation involves two separate activities. Creating value means finding trades where each side gives up what it values less for what it values more: a faster delivery for a lower price, flexibility on dates for a bigger order. This needs openness about what you care about. Claiming value means dividing what has been created, which rewards being firm and revealing little.

Lax and Sebenius called this the negotiator's dilemma: the behaviours that create value (candour, curiosity) leave you open to being exploited when it's time to divide, while the behaviours that claim value (concealing, anchoring high) shrink the pie and raise the risk of walking away with nothing. Most of the available gain comes from creating, so a sensible approach is to create first and claim second, rather than to claim from the start.

How to use it

  1. Know your priorities and your alternative before you start, so you can share what you value without giving up your position.
  2. Ask about interests, not just positions. "Why does that date matter?" often opens trades neither side saw.
  3. Trade across issues. Bundle several, so each side can give on what it cares less about.
  4. Then divide with objective standards (market rates, precedent, a fair-process rule) and be firm there. That's where claiming belongs.

Example

A client and a supplier haggle over price alone, and each hides its constraints. After one meeting, they share what matters: the client needs delivery before a trade show and cares less about packaging, while the supplier has spare capacity in the off-season but wants volume commitments. A deal at a slightly higher price, earlier delivery and a multi-year commitment leaves both better off than any split of the original price would.

Watch out for

  • Openness works only when it's mutual. If you share and the other side doesn't, trade information step by step.
  • Not every negotiation has a pie to grow. A one-off price on a commodity with a single issue is mostly claiming. The shape of this curve depends on how much hidden value there is.
