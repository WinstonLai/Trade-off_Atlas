Project management

Project management triangle

Scope, time and cost are tied together. Hold one fixed, change another, and the third must move. If nothing is allowed to move, quality pays.

Scopevs.Timevs.Cost

The trade-off

Every project has to fit an amount of work (scope) into the time and money (cost) available. Think of the work you can afford as roughly time × resources. If scope grows, either the timeline or the budget has to grow with it.

The fourth, hidden corner is quality. When a team is told to deliver more, sooner and cheaper all at once, the gap doesn't vanish. It turns into skipped testing, shortcuts and burnout.

How to use it

  1. Ask which corner is fixed. Is the deadline real (a launch event, a regulation) or just a preference? Is the budget capped? Usually only one corner is truly fixed.
  2. Pick the one that flexes. Agree up front which corner will move when things change. "If we find more work, the date moves, not the budget."
  3. Price every change request. When someone adds scope, answer with what it costs in time or money, not just "yes" or "no".
  4. Name quality out loud. If stakeholders refuse to move any corner, make it explicit that quality is now the variable.

Example

A team is building a customer portal for a fixed trade-show date with a fixed contractor budget. Halfway through, sales asks for single sign-on. With time and cost locked, the only honest answers are to drop other scope (say, the reporting page) to make room, or to accept that the demo will be rough.

Watch out for

  • Resources aren't instantly convertible. Adding money or people doesn't speed up work linearly. See Brooks's law.
  • Scope is the easiest corner to flex. Cutting nice-to-haves is usually cheaper than moving dates or budgets.
