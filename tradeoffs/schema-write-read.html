Schema-on-write vs. schema-on-read

Validate and shape data before it's stored (ETL) and consumers get clean tables. Store it raw and shape it later (ELT) and you stay flexible, but every reader pays.

Upfront rigourvs.Flexibility

The trade-off

With schema-on-write (classic ETL), data is checked and transformed before loading. Bad records are caught at the door, and everything in the warehouse is trustworthy, but a new field or a changed source needs pipeline work first.

With schema-on-read (ELT, data lakes), raw data lands untouched and structure is applied when someone queries it. Ingestion never breaks and history is preserved, but each consumer re-interprets the data, and problems surface later, in someone's dashboard.

How to use it

  1. Be strict where being wrong is expensive: finance, compliance and anything that's masked or audited.
  2. Be loose where the shape is still changing: new sources, logs, exploratory work.
  3. Layer it. Land raw data first, then promote it through cleaned and curated layers (bronze, silver, gold), tightening the contract at each step.
  4. Keep the raw copy. If a rule was wrong, you can rebuild without going back to the source.

Example

A source system starts sending prices as text ("12,50") instead of numbers. In an ETL pipeline the load fails at 2 a.m. and the on-call engineer fixes the mapping, so the data is late but right. In an ELT lake the load succeeds, and three weeks later a report shows revenue of zero for one region.

Watch out for

  • "Flexible" can become a swamp. A lake nobody documents is storage, not an asset.
  • Strictness can become a bottleneck. If every change waits for one team, people go around it.
  • It's a cousin of normalization vs. denormalization (where does the integrity work happen?) and affects how fresh your data can be.
